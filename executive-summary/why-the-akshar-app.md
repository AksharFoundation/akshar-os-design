# Why the Akshar App

Akshar Foundation · October 2026

Akshar’s model already works: individual children, peer learning, FLN, tablets, recycling, points, and a Government School Program (GSP) across more schools and districts. What does not work is how that model is run day to day.

Consent is on paper. Parents are informed only if someone reaches them. Teachers build routines and scan for struggling students by hand. Students use Kolibri for learning, then step out of the app for everything else. Staff training lives in another folder. Progress across schools is collated in Drive.

An app is preferred because those jobs need one shared record, not a chain of people, paper, and spreadsheets.

---

## Why an app is needed today

### 1. Parental consent has no audit trail

Consent is taken on paper. There is no reliable record of who consented, to what, when, or whether it was later withdrawn. Photos, student data, and any later use such as class-photo attendance need a trail that paper cannot keep. An app can record give and withdraw, with paper only as backup.

### 2. Parents are not informed about progress or school life

A parent should see their child’s progress in FLN, peer learning, digital, recycling, and leadership, and hear about notices, events, and assessments. Today that depends on whoever can reach them. An app lets the parent link their own child and see updates on the phone they already have.

### 3. Teachers set up classes and routines by hand

Teachers still assemble the day’s routine, activities, homework, and parent notices manually. An app makes class setup and routing the unit of work, instead of a stack of forms.

### 4. Teachers scan by hand for children who need attention

Remedial grouping is core to the model. Teachers currently notice who is falling behind themselves. An app can surface who needs attention and let the teacher form a remedial group from that view.

### 5. Kolibri covers learning; the rest of school does not

Students already have digital learning (Kolibri, Learn to Read). Homework, requesting a class, seeing progress, points, and teacher-assigned work still sit outside that app. The Akshar App is not a replacement for Kolibri. It is the layer that connects learning tools to the rest of the child’s school life — on the parent’s phone, often the only device in the house.

### 6. Staff learning and development is a separate pile

Akshar staff need 20 hours of training a year. Materials, registers, pre-work, progress, post-work, and certificates sit apart from school operations. An app can host L&D next to the work those staff are employed to do, including auto-issued certificates.

### 7. There is no single view across schools and geographies

Six data sets — students, assessments, attendance, recycling, peer learning, and points — are entered by district teams and merged by hand. That is slow, easy to get wrong, and invisible until the merge is done. An app gives each role a filtered dashboard (school, district, gender) from the same source, so Head Office does not become the database.

Those seven problems are one problem: **the model is shared; the system is not.** Paper and Drive cannot fix that. An app can.

---

## Why one app, not another folder

The same child has to be seen correctly by a parent, a teacher, a student, a GSP school team, Head Office, and the organisation. That only works if there is one product with three ways in.

| View | Who | Why the app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Individual** | Parent, teacher, student | Consent trail, progress and notices at home, class routine and remedial groups, Kolibri plus the rest of school in one place |
| **Team** | GSP (local school) and Head Office | Structured data, role-based access, L&D, school and HO dashboards |
| **Organisation** | The network | One view across schools and geographies |

Role-based access already matches how GSP is staffed: leadership (DPM, APM, ADPM, CPM), mid-level (Cluster Coordinator), and data entry (Schools Coordinator, Schools Assistant). A parent sees only their own child.

The six data sets the app has to hold — because they are already being entered, just not in one place:

| Data set | What it captures |
| --- | --- |
| Students | Name, class, roll no, gender, caste, religion; add, delete, auto-promote after 31 March |
| Assessments | Baseline, plus weekly self-paced tests for selected students |
| Attendance | FLN, digital and tablet, vocational training |
| Recycling | Plastic collection, including eco-bricks |
| Peer learning | Activity records |
| All points | Activity, student-leader, and special points, auto-totalled |

Today those records live in unstructured Google Drive. An app puts them in a proper database, with access control, backups, and a consent trail — which is what GSP needs now that it spans multiple schools in multiple locations.
